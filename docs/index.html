Scripture Media Player

Listening to trial scripture media

A player for scripture audio and video that has not yet been consultant-approved. Someone sends you a bundle file; it plays only on your device, only for the number of plays they set. No internet connection is needed to listen.

Is this content ready to share? No. Trial content is under review. Please don't copy it, forward it, post it, or play it for a wider group than the person who sent it intended. The limits in the app are a reminder, not a substitute for that trust.

Download

The player runs on Android phones and tablets, and on Windows and Linux computers. Every platform reads the same .smbundle files.

Pre-release builds. Releases marked Pre-release are for testing. They work, but please report anything odd rather than passing them on — and expect to uninstall before installing the next one on Android.

Your phone will warn you before installing. This app isn't distributed through the Google Play Store, so Android asks you to confirm that you trust the file. Tap the downloaded .apk, and when Android says it can't install unknown apps, choose Settings → turn on Allow from this source → Back → Install. You only do this once.

Getting started

  1. Install the app

    Download it from the buttons above and install. Nothing happens on first launch except that the app shows you your device ID — a short code that identifies this one device.

  2. Send your device ID to whoever is preparing the content

    Open the app and tap the copy or share button next to the device ID. Send it to them however you normally message them. They need it before they can build your bundle — the content is locked to the specific devices they list.

    The device ID isn't secret and doesn't identify you personally. It's just a number your device already reports. It changes if the device is factory-reset — if that happens, send the new one.

  3. Receive the .smbundle file

    They'll send you a file ending in .smbundle — by WhatsApp, email, a memory card, a USB cable, or whatever works where you are. Save it to your device.

  4. Open it

    Tap the file in your file manager or in the chat app, and choose Scripture Media Player. The app checks that the bundle is meant for your device, unpacks it, and shows the playlist. This can take a minute for a large bundle.

    On a computer, double-click the .smbundle file, or open the app and use Load bundle.

  5. Listen or watch

    Tap any item to play. Turn the phone sideways during a video to go full-screen. The app remembers where you stopped and picks up there next time.

About the play limits

The person who built your bundle chooses the limits. Depending on what they set, you may see:

What you'll seeWhat it means
A play count on each item Each item can be played a set number of times before it pauses for a while.
“Available again in …” The limit for that item is used up for now. It comes back on its own after the reset period — often a day. You don't need to do anything.
“Please wait before playing again” A required gap between plays, so material is heard attentively rather than left looping.
A limited number of items per session You can move through only so many different items in one sitting.
“This bundle has expired” The bundle reached its end date, or its lifetime play limit. Ask for a new bundle.

A video that shows its first frame but won't play is almost always a limit that's been reached or a bundle that's expired — not a broken file. Check the message under the player.

Getting a new bundle later

When new material is ready, they'll send a newer .smbundle. Open it the same way. The new bundle replaces the old one, and the old content is removed from your device.

You can't go back to a bundle you've already finished with. Once you move to a newer bundle, re-opening an older one is refused. This is deliberate — it stops play limits from being reset by reloading an old file. If you need old material again, ask for a fresh bundle of it.

If something goes wrong

MessageWhat to do
“This device is not authorized” The bundle was built for other devices, or your device ID changed (usually a factory reset). Send your current device ID and ask for a rebuilt bundle.
“This bundle was previously used” You've already moved on to a newer bundle. Ask for a fresh one.
“Insufficient storage space” Free up space and open the file again. A bundle needs roughly twice its own size free while it unpacks.
Tapping the file doesn't offer the player Some chat apps hand over files in a way Android doesn't recognise. Save the file to your device first, then open it from the Files or My Files app.
Nothing plays and there's no message Close the app fully and reopen it. If it persists, tell whoever sent the bundle — the app can write an import log they can look at.

Don't uninstall the app to fix a problem. Uninstalling erases your imported content and your place in it, and you'll need a new bundle. Ask first.

Preparing content to send

If you're the one distributing trial material, you use the bundler — a desktop application that packs your audio and video into a single .smbundle file, locked to the devices you name and carrying the limits you choose.

  1. Collect device IDs

    Ask each person to install the player and send you the device ID it shows them. Bundles are built for a specific list of devices; a device that isn't on the list can't open the file.

  2. Add your media

    MP3, WAV, and M4A for audio; MP4, WebM, AVI, and MOV for video. Order them the way you want them to appear in the playlist.

  3. Choose the limits

    Plays per item per period, and how long that period is. Optionally: a required gap between plays, a lifetime cap, a bundle expiry date, and limits on how many items can be worked through in one session.

    Set these deliberately for the first bundle you send someone. Limits that are too tight get read as a broken app — the most common support question is a video that won't play, which is nearly always a limit doing its job.

  4. Build and send

    The bundler writes one .smbundle file. Send it however suits your context — messaging app, email, memory card, or USB. It carries its own protection, so the delivery channel doesn't need to be a secure one.

What the protection does and doesn't do

Be honest with reviewers about this, so the limits are understood as a shared agreement rather than a lock.

Protects againstDoes not protect against
Forwarding the bundle to someone else — it won't open on an unlisted device.

Casual copying of media off the device.

Resetting limits by reloading an older bundle.

Unlimited listening — plays are counted and capped. 		Screen or audio recording while it plays.

A rooted or jailbroken device.

Changing the device clock to move a reset along.
